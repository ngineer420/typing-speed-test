    Endurance, not a burst

    Most typing tests are over before anything interesting happens. Five minutes is long enough for the things that actually degrade real typing to show up: your wrists drift, your shoulders creep upward, you stop reading a word ahead, and the error rate that was invisible in the first thirty seconds starts compounding. Almost everyone scores lower here than on a one-minute test, and the size of that drop is the number worth paying attention to — it is a direct read on how much of your speed survives contact with sustained work.

    This is also the length that matches the jobs people take typing tests for. Transcription, live captioning, support-queue work, and data entry are all measured in sustained output over minutes and hours, not in one heroic sprint. A five-minute score that holds steady is worth far more to an employer than a fifteen-second score twice as high.

    The deck below still offers the shorter durations if you want to warm up first, but this page starts on the full five minutes on purpose, and your five-minute bests are kept separately from your short-run bests so the two never get quietly compared. Pace yourself: the goal is a flat line, not a fast start. If you want the technique side of that, see how to type faster.
